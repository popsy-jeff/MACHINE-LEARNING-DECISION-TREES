THERE ARE USUALLY SITUATIONS WHERE DECISION TREES AND RANDOM FORESTS ARE USED AS COMPARED TO LOGISTIC REGRESSION


Decision Tree

Captures non-linear relationships and threshold-based rules (e.g., "if age > 30 and income > $50k") without manual feature engineering.
Automatically detects interactions between variables by splitting on combinations.
Handles mixed data types, missing values, and outliers with minimal preprocessing.
Produces a visual, flowchart-style output that's easy to explain to non-technical people.

Random Forest

Improves on a single tree's accuracy and stability by averaging many trees, reducing overfitting and variance.
Scales well to large datasets with many features, capturing complex nonlinear patterns automatically.
Best when prediction accuracy is the priority over interpretability.
Commonly used for messy, real-world problems like fraud detection, churn, or risk scoring.

Logistic Regression

Best when you need to quantify each variable's exact contribution (e.g., odds ratios, coefficients).
Works well on small datasets with a roughly linear relationship, with low risk of overfitting.
Fast to train, easy to update, and computationally cheap.
Preferred in regulated fields (finance, healthcare) requiring transparency, auditability, and well-calibrated probabilities.
